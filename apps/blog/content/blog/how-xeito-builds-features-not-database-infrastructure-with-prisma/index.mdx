[Xeito](https://www.xeito.app) started as a small script for coordinating squash matches in Malta. Less than a year later, Gabriel Gil Graña is building a data-heavy platform for racket-sports clubs, teams, and players.

For Gabriel, the job is to spend time on the workflows players and clubs actually feel.

Prisma gives Xeito a typed data layer and managed database defaults, so Gabriel can keep shipping product features instead of becoming the infrastructure team.

![Gregory Boch interviewing Gabriel Gil Graña about building Xeito with Prisma](/how-xeito-builds-features-not-database-infrastructure-with-prisma/imgs/gabriel-interview.jpg)

## Xeito at a glance

- **Product:** A racket-sports platform for clubs, teams, and players.
- **Founder:** Gabriel Gil Graña, building from Malta.
- **Use case:** Fast-changing product data, live match updates, standings, registrations, and payments.
- **Stack:** Next.js on Vercel, Prisma ORM, and Prisma Postgres.
- **Prisma role:** Keep schema, migrations, and typed queries close to the app code.
- **Database defaults:** Pooling, backups, storage, and operational patterns handled by default, with room to tune when needed.

## About Xeito

Xeito helps racket-sports communities manage the work around a match: availability, rosters, leagues, scores, standings, registrations, payments, and events.

The first version solved a local coordination problem for squash players in Malta. Then the idea kept expanding into a platform for players, captains, clubs, coaches, and tournament organizers.

Gabriel has already seen interest beyond Malta, including the UK and Spain, and beyond squash into sports like padel, pickleball, tennis, and table tennis.

## The challenge: live sports data on a small team

Sports admin looks simple until the product has to model everything happening around the court:

- players, clubs, teams, and rosters
- calendars, availability, lineups, and matches
- points, standings, registrations, and payments
- notifications, live scoring, and live streams

The product also has real-time pressure. During league nights, referees submit points as matches happen. Every point can change the standings while players follow along live.

For a small team, that kind of product creates an uncomfortable tradeoff. You can spend time on features, or you can spend time configuring infrastructure.

<Quotes speakerName="Gabriel Gil Graña">
I really wanted to spend time creating features and not having to deal with memory, pool connections, storage limits, replicas, backups.
</Quotes>

Those tasks matter. They are also not the product. Gabriel wanted a path where the default decisions were already good enough to keep building, with room to understand and tune the system when needed.

## Why Prisma

Gabriel had used Prisma professionally before, but Xeito was the first time he used Prisma for the data layer in one of his own products. The deciding factor was not a long checklist. It was the way Prisma keeps the data model visible, explicit, and connected to the application.

<Quotes speakerName="Gabriel Gil Graña">
Prisma for me was the winner because of the schema. The way of modeling the data kept very, very tight with the code.
</Quotes>

With [Prisma ORM](https://www.prisma.io/docs/orm), the schema becomes the place where Gabriel defines how the product works. The model translates into migrations, and the generated client keeps application code strongly typed as the product changes.

That matters for Xeito because the product model keeps evolving. A tournament workflow adds registrations and schedules. A live scoring workflow adds points and standings. An AI coaching workflow will add match analysis, statistics, heat maps, and recommendations.

[Prisma Postgres](https://www.prisma.io/docs/postgres) gives Gabriel a managed path for the database layer, while Prisma ORM keeps the model close to the code. The combination lets him spend more time on Xeito and less time managing database operations by hand.

## What Xeito can build now

In the demo, Xeito already looked far beyond a scheduling tool.

![Xeito club page showing Malta Squash, members, and a league entry](/how-xeito-builds-features-not-database-infrastructure-with-prisma/imgs/xeito-club.png)

![Xeito league page showing standings, recent match results, and league metadata](/how-xeito-builds-features-not-database-infrastructure-with-prisma/imgs/xeito-league.png)

Today, Xeito can already support the core workflows around racket sports:

- clubs manage members and leagues
- players follow standings and match results
- organizers run registrations, payments, reminders, and schedules
- referees submit points from the court as standings update live

The technical proof is in the live referee workflow. Xeito sends many queries that need fast responses while players are watching the standings change.

<Quotes speakerName="Gabriel Gil Graña">
Prisma has always been able to cope quite well without me having to worry about anything that happens in the background.
</Quotes>

That is the value of boring infrastructure. The product can become more ambitious without forcing the founder to spend his time on database operations.

For Xeito, that means more attention on the workflows people touch: clubs, leagues, payments, tournaments, and live scoring. Prisma handles the data layer underneath, while Gabriel keeps improving the product experience above it.

## What comes next

Gabriel is building toward a fuller platform for racket sports.

Next comes tournament software: brackets, schedules, venue screens, organizer controls, and notifications. After that comes AI coaching: match analysis, player statistics, heat maps, and recommendations.

Both depend on the same thing: a data layer that stays easy to change as the product gets more ambitious. Prisma gives Gabriel a default path that keeps those changes grounded in the schema and connected to the code.

## Keep Building Features

Xeito is a good reminder that "small team" does not have to mean "small product."

The hard part is choosing where the team spends its attention. For Gabriel, the answer is clear: work on features, make the product better for players and clubs, and let Prisma handle the database defaults that every serious product needs.

That is the platform promise Prisma should make to builders: make me successful by default, keep the escape hatches visible, and let me keep building.

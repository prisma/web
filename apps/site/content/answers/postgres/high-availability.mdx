Add high availability when a few minutes of database downtime would cost you more than a second server running all year, or when you have promised your customers more uptime than your database host promises you. Before that point, the outages you are most likely to see come from changes, mistakes and an overloaded app, and a standby doesn't prevent them.

## What is PostgreSQL high availability?

PostgreSQL high availability is a setup where a standby server keeps a live copy of the primary database and takes over if the primary fails. The primary streams its write-ahead log, the record of every change, to the standby through [streaming replication](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/warm-standby.html#STREAMING-REPLICATION). PostgreSQL itself [doesn't detect a failed primary](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/current/warm-standby-failover.html), so a separate tool or the hosting provider watches the primary and promotes the standby to primary when the primary stops answering.

### Synchronous or asynchronous?

The standby can be synchronous or asynchronous. With a synchronous standby, the primary waits until the standby has saved each committed change before it confirms the commit to your app, so a failover doesn't lose committed data. Every write takes longer as a result. An asynchronous standby keeps writes fast, and a failover can lose the last changes that hadn't reached it yet.

## How long does a failover take?

A failover is a short outage of its own. Amazon RDS documents failovers as [typically under 35 seconds](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/multi-az-db-clusters-concepts-failover.html) for Multi-AZ DB clusters and [typically 60 to 120 seconds](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Concepts.MultiAZ.Failover.html) for Multi-AZ DB instances. In both cases your app has to re-establish its connections afterwards. Azure Database for PostgreSQL documents [60 to 120 seconds](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/postgresql/high-availability/concepts-high-availability) for zone-redundant failover, and says a busy server can take longer.

Planned maintenance works the same way. Azure applies minor version upgrades to the standby first, then fails over. Your app is unavailable during that failover.

## What doesn't high availability protect against?

A standby protects you from one kind of failure: the primary's server breaking. Most outages have other causes. Google's SRE team found that [roughly 70% of outages](https://sre.google/sre-book/introduction/) are due to changes in a live system.

- **Bad changes to your app.** A broken deploy or configuration change takes your app down while the database and its standby stay healthy.
- **Mistakes in your data.** A mistaken `DELETE` or a bad migration reaches the standby within moments. Azure's documentation says [you can't use the standby](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/postgresql/high-availability/concepts-high-availability) to recover from an accidental drop of a table or incorrect updates, and that you need a restore from backup instead.
- **An overloaded app.** A full connection pool, where every database connection is in use, or a slow query can stop your app while the database server keeps running.
- **Regional outages.** On Amazon RDS Multi-AZ and Azure, the standby runs in the same region as the primary, so a regional outage stops both servers.

## Do you need high availability?

Not for most new apps. You are ready for it when one of these is true:

- A few minutes of downtime would cost you more than a second server running all year.
- A contract promises your customers more uptime than your database host promises you.

Until then, these protections cover more of the failures you are likely to have:

1. **Backups you have restored.** Restore one to a new database before you need it, as the [recovery practice guide](/postgres/practice-database-recovery) shows.
2. **An app that reconnects.** Retry a dropped connection with a short delay, so a restart or a failover on any host causes a short burst of errors, not a stuck app.
3. **A health check.** Alert someone when the app can't reach the database.

The [small production app guide](/postgres/small-production-app-postgres) lists which hosts include high availability and on which plans.

## Where does Prisma Postgres fit?

[Prisma Postgres](/docs/postgres) is managed PostgreSQL. It doesn't run a standby server or fail over to one. It backs up every database to separate storage and keeps adding your latest changes in small batches. If your database stops responding, Prisma Postgres notices and restarts it. If a server fails for good, we rebuild your database from its backups on another server in the same region. You could lose only the most recent changes that hadn't been backed up off the server yet.

- **Backups on separate storage.** Prisma Postgres backs up every database to separate storage, away from the server that runs it. It keeps adding your latest changes to those backups in small batches.
- **Backups you can restore yourself.** On the Starter, Pro and Business plans, a [daily backup](/docs/postgres/database/backups) is available on each day that your database has activity. Starter and Pro keep the last 7 days, and Business keeps the last 30. You can restore one from the Console or with the Prisma CLI. A restore brings your data back as it was when the backup ran. Point-in-time recovery is coming in November 2026.
- **Automatic restarts.** If your database stops responding, Prisma Postgres notices and restarts it.
- **Recovery from a lost server.** If a server fails for good, we rebuild your database from its backups on another server in the same region. Once the rebuild starts, most of its time goes to moving your data, so a larger database takes longer.
- **An uptime commitment.** On the Pro, Business and Enterprise plans, the [service level agreement](https://www.prisma.io/legal/sla) commits to at least 99.95% uptime each month.

Your app keeps its Postgres client, whether that is `pg`, Drizzle, Kysely or Prisma ORM. Prisma ORM is optional.

## Where is Prisma Postgres the wrong choice?

For an app that needs a standby with automatic failover today. Prisma Postgres doesn't run a standby server or fail over to one (its [docs](/docs/postgres) list none, checked November 2, 2026). It also rebuilds a lost database within one region. If your app needs more than the uptime the service level agreement commits to, [contact us](https://www.prisma.io/contact), or choose a host from the [small production app guide](/postgres/small-production-app-postgres#high-availability) that offers a standby.

The Free plan doesn't include backups you can restore yourself. If you are on Free and need them, [contact us](https://www.prisma.io/contact).
